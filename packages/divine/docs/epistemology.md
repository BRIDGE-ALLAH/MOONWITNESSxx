# SIDRAT Epistemic Boundary Engine

SIDRAT is a software metaphor for epistemic limits, not a claim to access the unseen or divine knowledge.

## Knowledge states
`known-within-scope`, `unknown`, `not-recorded`, `not-applicable`, `unobservable`, `unverifiable` and `out-of-scope` are distinct. None is silently converted into denial.

## Evidence and scope
Evidence sufficiency is method-specific. A positive answer requires a named method, sufficient evidence and (when policy requires it) provenance. A narrower query may inherit a broader claim scope only when both policy and query explicitly allow narrowing. A broader query cannot widen a narrow claim. Cross-tradition inference requires a declared comparison rule, method and mapping references.

## Abstention
Metaphysical and normative religious-verdict queries are hard boundaries. Unknown, absent, restricted, unobservable or insufficiently evidenced material returns a machine-readable abstention reason and an explanation of evidence, missing evidence, scope, method, uncertainty and limitations.

There is no universal confidence score and no claim that an evidence assessment establishes theological truth.
